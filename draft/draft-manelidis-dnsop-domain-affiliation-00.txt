                 Domain Affiliation Confirmation (DAC)
              draft-manelidis-dnsop-domain-affiliation-00

Abstract

   Security systems assess domain names one at a time.  A new domain of
   an established organization therefore starts with no reputation, and
   a legitimate domain that resembles another organization's name can be
   reported as an impersonation.  This document defines Domain
   Affiliation Confirmation (DAC), by which the operator of a parent
   domain confirms that a child domain is operated by it or under its
   authority.  The child publishes a DNS record naming the parent and a
   random token; the parent confirms that token, in the DNS or over
   HTTPS, by naming the child.  Verification takes at most three DNS
   queries.  The parent publishes no list of its domains, and an
   affiliation is ended by deleting one record.  The document also
   states what a confirmation means to relying parties such as takedown
   services and reputation systems.

1.  Introduction

1.1.  Problem Statement

   Mail filters, browser warning systems, secure web gateways, and
   brand-protection services evaluate domain names individually.  Two
   consequences follow for organizations that operate more than one
   domain.

   New domains start without reputation.  When an organization launches
      a product or service under a new domain name, that name is treated
      as unknown, and often as suspicious, because nothing tells the
      evaluating system that it is operated by the same organization as
      an established domain.

   Legitimate look-alikes are reported as impersonations.
      Brand-protection services search for newly registered names that
      resemble their clients' names.  A legitimate domain of one
      organization can resemble the name of an unrelated organization.
      The service has no standard way to learn that the domain is
      operated by an established organization and may ask the domain's
      registrar or hosting provider to suspend it.  Erroneous takedown
      requests can disable an entire legitimate domain [ITCHIO].

   Both problems would be reduced if an evaluating system could ask a
   precise question and get an answer from the party able to give it:
   "Does the operator of this established domain confirm that it
   operates this other domain?"  No deployed mechanism answers it.
   Registration data [RFC9083] is frequently redacted and was not
   designed to express relationships between domains, and certificates
   bind keys to names, not names to each other.

1.2.  This Document

   This document defines Domain Affiliation Confirmation (DAC).  The
   domain to be affiliated (the child) publishes a DNS record that names
   its parent domain and carries a random token.  The parent confirms
   the affiliation by publishing, under a name derived from that token,
   a record that names the child.  A verifier reads the child's record,
   reads the parent's confirmation, and checks that the confirmation
   names the child it started from.

   The document also states what a confirmation means (Section 8): the
   parent's operator indicates that it operates the child or authorizes
   its operation, and that reports of abuse concerning the child may be
   directed to it.  A relationship that relying parties are not meant to
   act on would leave the problems above unsolved (Appendix A.9).

1.3.  Design Goals

   1.  Only the parent can create an affiliation.  A claim the parent
       has not confirmed has no effect, and a confirmation for one child
       cannot be reused for another.

   2.  The parent publishes nothing from which its affiliated domains
       can be listed.  Each child's claim is public, so affiliated
       domains can be found by examining domains individually, as with
       any public DNS data.

   3.  An affiliation is ended by deleting one record at the parent,
       without any change at the child.  The change takes effect within
       cache and DNSSEC signature lifetimes (Section 11.6).

   4.  No new key material.  The protocol relies on the parent's
       existing control of its DNS zone or, optionally, its web origin.

   5.  Bounded cost.  Verification takes at most three TXT queries, plus
       one HTTPS request and its address resolution when the parent uses
       that channel, with no recursion and no searching.

   6.  Deployable with existing tools.  Records are TXT records at
       underscored names [RFC8552].

   7.  The verification procedure does not consult a list of public
       suffixes.  Every confirmation requires a record that only the
       parent's operator can publish.

1.4.  Non-Goals

   DAC does not establish the identity, legitimacy, or licensing of the
   parent's operator.  It conveys a statement by whoever operates the
   parent domain; whether that party deserves trust is for each relying
   party to decide (Section 8).

   DAC does not express relationships between independent organizations,
   such as partnerships or service providers (Appendix A.5).

   DAC does not define where administrative boundaries in the DNS lie.
   Relying parties may still need such information to decide which names
   to evaluate.

1.5.  Relationship to Other Work

   [I-D.ietf-dnsop-domain-verification-techniques] describes how a
   service verifies that a user controls a domain.  DAC follows its
   practices for TXT records at underscored names and for tokens, but
   records a standing relationship between two domains rather than
   proving control of one domain to one service.

   [I-D.ietf-regext-epp-same-entity] lets a registry enforce that a
   group of names in that registry is held by one registrant.  DAC
   operates across registries and is published by the domains'
   operators.

2.  Terminology

   DNS terms are used as defined in [RFC9499].  This document also uses
   the following terms.

   Child domain:  The domain name whose affiliation is declared or
      verified.

   Parent domain:  The domain name that the child domain names as its
      parent.

   Parent operator:  The party that controls the content of the parent




Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                 [Page 6]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


      domain's DNS zone or, for confirmations over HTTPS, of the parent
      domain's HTTPS origin.

   Claim:  The TXT record published by the child domain (Section 4.2).

   Marker:  The TXT record by which a parent domain indicates that it
      participates and selects a confirmation channel (Section 4.4).

   Confirmation:  The record by which the parent domain confirms one
      claim, published in the DNS (Section 4.5) or over HTTPS
      (Section 7).

   Token:  A random value, unique to one child domain, that links a
      claim to its confirmation (Section 4.6).

   Verifier:  A party that carries out the procedure in Section 6.

   Relying party:  A party that makes decisions using verification
      results, such as a takedown service, a mail filter, or a
      reputation system.

   In examples, "bank.example" is the parent domain and "embank.example"
   is the child domain [RFC2606].  The token used in examples,
   "mfrggzdfmztwq2lknnwg23tpobyxe43u", is illustrative and not random.

3.  Protocol Overview

   An affiliation consists of three records.  The claim is published by
   the child; the marker and the confirmation are published by the
   parent.

    Child: embank.example              Parent: bank.example
   +-------------------------------+  +--------------------------------+
   | _affiliation.embank.example.  |  | _affiliation.bank.example.     |
   |   TXT "v=DAC1;                |  |   TXT "v=DAC1"        (marker) |
   |     p=bank.example;           |  |                                |
   |     t=<token>"        (claim) |  | <token>._affiliation.          |
   +-------------------------------+  |        bank.example.           |
                                      |   TXT "v=DAC1;                 |
                                      |     d=embank.example"          |
                                      |                 (confirmation) |
                                      +--------------------------------+

   Verifier:
    1. TXT _affiliation.embank.example  -> p=bank.example, t=<token>
    2. TXT _affiliation.bank.example    -> marker, channel "dns"
    3. TXT <token>._affiliation.bank.example
                                        -> d=embank.example
    4. d= equals the child domain       -> result "confirmed"

                  Figure 1: Records and Verification Steps

   A claim copied to another domain fails because the confirmation names
   the original child.  Confirmation names cannot be found without the
   corresponding claims, because they contain the token.  The marker
   prevents a wildcard elsewhere in the parent's zone from answering for
   confirmation names (Section 11.7) and tells the verifier which
   channel the parent uses.

4.  Records

4.1.  Common Syntax

   The three records share one tag-list syntax: a version tag followed
   by zero or more tags, separated by semicolons.  The ABNF [RFC5234]
   below uses case-sensitive strings [RFC7405]; WSP, ALPHA, and DIGIT
   are the core rules of [RFC5234].















Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                 [Page 8]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


   <CODE BEGINS> file "dac.abnf"
   record      = *WSP v-tag *( *WSP ";" *WSP tag ) [ *WSP ";" ] *WSP
   v-tag       = %s"v" *WSP "=" *WSP %s"DAC1"
   tag         = tag-name *WSP "=" *WSP tag-value
   tag-name    = ALPHA *( ALPHA / DIGIT / "_" )
   tag-value   = 1*tval-char
   tval-char   = %x21-3A / %x3C-7E        ; VCHAR except ";"

   p-value     = domain                   ; claim: parent domain
   t-value     = 26*52b32-char            ; claim: token
   c-value     = 1*tval-char              ; marker: channel
   m-value     = 1*tval-char              ; marker: mode
   r-value     = 1*tval-char              ; marker: abuse contact URI
   d-value     = domain                   ; confirmation: child domain
   s-value     = 1*tval-char              ; confirmation: scope
   x-value     = 4DIGIT "-" 2DIGIT "-" 2DIGIT ; confirmation: expiry
   f-value     = 1*tval-char              ; marker: report URI

   domain      = label *( "." label )
   label       = ld-char [ *61ldh-char ld-char ]
   ld-char     = %x61-7A / DIGIT          ; lowercase letters and digits
   ldh-char    = ld-char / "-"
   b32-char    = %x61-7A / %x32-37        ; RFC 4648 base32, lowercase
   <CODE ENDS>

   A TXT record is a DAC record if its text, after any leading spaces or
   tabs, begins with "v", optional whitespace, "=", optional whitespace,
   and "DAC1", followed by whitespace, ";", or the end of the text.
   Other TXT records are ignored, so DAC records can share a name with
   other TXT records.  A DAC record is well-formed if it matches the
   "record" rule, contains no tag name more than once, and the value of
   every tag defined in this document matches the rule given for it,
   with an "x" value that is also a valid calendar date.  A DAC record
   that is not well-formed is malformed.

   *  Tag names are case-sensitive.  Verifiers MUST ignore tags they do
      not recognize.  New tags are registered as described in
      Section 13.3.1; adding a tag does not change the version.

   *  A TXT record consisting of several character-strings is
      interpreted as their concatenation without separators, as in
      Section 3.3 of [RFC7208].  Publishers SHOULD keep each record
      within a single character-string of at most 255 octets.

   *  Domain names in records are in canonical form: A-labels [RFC5890]
      in lowercase, without a trailing dot, at most 253 octets long,
      with a last label that is not entirely numeric.  A label with
      hyphens in both the third and fourth positions MUST be a valid



Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                 [Page 9]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


      A-label.  The "domain" rule expresses the character-level
      constraints; a name violating the others is malformed.  Verifiers
      do not repair names.

4.2.  Claim Record

   A child domain declares its parent with one TXT record at the name
   formed by prepending the label "_affiliation" to the child domain:

   _affiliation.embank.example. 3600 IN TXT
       "v=DAC1; p=bank.example; t=mfrggzdfmztwq2lknnwg23tpobyxe43u"

   p (required):  The parent domain, matching the "p-value" rule.

   t (required):  The token, matching the "t-value" rule (Section 4.6).

   A child domain has at most one parent and MUST NOT publish more than
   one DAC record at its "_affiliation" name; a domain that is also a
   parent includes its marker tags in the same record (Section 4.4).
   The parent domain MUST NOT be the child domain itself.  The claim
   name MAY be a CNAME, for example when the child's records are managed
   by the parent's operator.

4.3.  Scope of an Affiliation

   A claim is published at, and an affiliation is verified for, one
   domain name.  Organizations typically publish claims at their
   registrable domains, such as "embank.example", not at every host
   name.

   A relying party assessing a name below such a domain, for example
   "marketing.embank.example", determines the domain it regards as
   responsible for that name, as it already does when attributing
   reputation, and verifies that domain.  Claims are not published at
   names like "marketing.embank.example" for this purpose.

   By default, a confirmation states nothing about names below the child
   domain, and relying parties apply their usual practice to them.  A
   parent that operates, or authorizes the operation of, every name
   below the child states this by including the "s" tag with the value
   "subtree" in the confirmation (Section 4.5).  The confirmation then
   also covers every name below the child.  A parent MUST NOT include it
   when names below the child are operated by parties not acting under
   the parent's authority, such as customers of a hosting or platform
   service (Section 11.12).

   If a name below a child with a subtree confirmation publishes its own
   claim, that claim governs the name and the names below it.

4.4.  Marker Record

   A parent domain that confirms affiliations publishes exactly one DAC
   record at the name formed by prepending "_affiliation" to the parent
   domain.  A domain that is both a child and a parent publishes a
   single record there containing both the claim tags and the marker
   tags.

   _affiliation.bank.example. 3600 IN TXT
       "v=DAC1; r=mailto:abuse@bank.example"

   c (optional):  The confirmation channel.  This document defines
      "dns", the default (Section 4.5), and "https" (Section 7).  Other
      values are registered as described in Section 13.3.2.  With
      "https", DNS confirmation records are not consulted.

   r (optional):  A URI [RFC3986] for reports of abuse concerning the
      parent's affiliated domains, with scheme "mailto" [RFC6068] or
      "https".  Semicolons and whitespace in the URI are percent-
      encoded.  Verifiers ignore an "r" tag with any other scheme.

   m (optional):  The mode: "enforce", the default, or "test".  In
      "test" mode the parent is deploying the protocol and asks that its
      confirmations not be acted on (Section 10.3).  Verifiers treat any
      value other than "enforce" as "test".

   f (optional):  A URI to which verifiers may send reports of claims
      naming this parent that were not confirmed (Section 9).  It is
      either "https://" followed by a host and an optional path, without
      userinfo, port, query, or fragment, or "mailto:" followed by
      exactly one address, without header fields.  The host, or the
      domain of the address, is in canonical form (Section 4.1) and is
      the parent domain or a name below it.  Verifiers ignore an "f" tag
      that does not meet these conditions.

   The marker MUST be a TXT record owned by the "_affiliation" name
   itself, not data reached through a CNAME or DNAME record.  The marker
   makes "_affiliation" an existing node in the parent zone, so that a
   wildcard higher in the zone cannot answer for names below it
   (Section 11.7).  The "_affiliation" name MAY be delegated as a
   separate zone, in which case that zone's operator publishes the
   marker and confirmations.  A DNAME record [RFC6672] at that name
   redirects the queries for confirmations to another zone and has the
   same effect as delegating them.







Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 11]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


4.5.  Confirmation Record (DNS Channel)

   For each confirmed child, a parent using the DNS channel publishes
   one TXT record at the name formed by prepending the child's token to
   the parent's "_affiliation" name:

   mfrggzdfmztwq2lknnwg23tpobyxe43u._affiliation.bank.example.
       3600 IN TXT "v=DAC1; d=embank.example"

   d (required):  The child domain, matching the "d-value" rule.

   s (optional):  The scope.  The value "subtree" states that the
      confirmation also covers every name below the child domain
      (Section 4.3).  Verifiers ignore any other value; other scopes
      would be defined with a new tag.

   x (optional):  The last day of validity, as a full-date in UTC
      [RFC3339], matching the "x-value" rule.  The confirmation applies
      until the end of that day, 23:59:59 UTC, and not afterwards.
      Without "x", the confirmation applies until it is removed.

   An expiry date suits domains used for a limited time, such as
   campaign sites, and also limits the consequences of a confirmation
   that is not removed when its domain changes hands (Section 11.4), for
   example when it is set to the domain's registration expiry date and
   updated on renewal.  A parent extends a confirmation by replacing the
   record with one carrying a later date.  Registration expiry dates
   normally move forward when domains renew automatically, so a parent
   tying "x" to them updates the confirmation at each renewal.

   A confirmation name MUST NOT hold more than one DAC record.  It MAY
   be a CNAME, for example to a name hosted by a DNS provider.  The
   parent MUST NOT publish wildcard records at or below its
   "_affiliation" name.

4.6.  Tokens

   A token links one claim to one confirmation.  It makes confirmation
   names unpredictable, so that a parent's confirmations cannot be found
   by guessing names, whether by online queries or, for DNSSEC-signed
   zones using NSEC3, by offline guessing of hashed names.  A token is
   not a secret: it is published in the claim and appears in queries.
   The protocol's resistance to forged affiliations does not depend on
   tokens being unknown (Section 11.3).







Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 12]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


   *  A token MUST contain at least 128 bits, and SHOULD contain 160
      bits, from a cryptographically secure random source [RFC4086].  At
      128 bits, guessing a confirmation name is infeasible and
      collisions between tokens are negligible.

   *  A token is encoded in base32 (Section 6 of [RFC4648]) with
      lowercase letters and without padding, giving 26 to 52 characters;
      a 160-bit token is 32 characters, well within the 63-octet limit
      on a DNS label [RFC1035].  Case-sensitive encodings such as base64
      cannot be used, because DNS name matching ignores case [RFC4343]
      and some resolvers alter the case of query names.

   *  Tokens are opaque; verifiers do not decode them.  A parent MUST
      use a different token for each child and SHOULD use a new token
      when re-establishing an affiliation that was previously ended.

5.  Publishing an Affiliation

5.1.  Creating an Affiliation

   1.  The parent operator publishes the marker, once, before its first
       affiliation.

   2.  The parent operator generates a token for the child and publishes
       the confirmation (Section 4.5 or Section 7).

   3.  The child's operator publishes the claim.  When parent and child
       are operated by the same organization, one tool performs all of
       these steps (Section 10.1).

   Publishing the confirmation first lets the affiliation verify as soon
   as the claim is visible.

5.2.  Ending an Affiliation

   The parent operator ends an affiliation by removing the confirmation;
   no change at the child is needed.  A parent operator MUST remove the
   confirmation for a child domain that it no longer operates or
   authorizes, including when the child domain is transferred, sold, or
   allowed to expire, except as described in Section 5.3.  A claim left
   in the child's zone, or copied there by a later registrant, has no
   effect once the confirmation is gone.

   A parent that withdraws from the protocol removes its marker, which
   ends all of its affiliations.






Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 13]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


5.3.  Moving a Child to Another Parent

   When a child domain moves from one parent to another, for example
   after an acquisition, the operators proceed in this order so that the
   child remains confirmed throughout:

   1.  The new parent publishes its marker, if it has none, and a
       confirmation for the child under a new token.

   2.  The child replaces its claim with one naming the new parent and
       the new token.

   3.  After verifying that the child's claim names the new parent, and
       after the TTL of the old claim has passed (86400 seconds if the
       old parent does not know it), the old parent removes its
       confirmation.

   If the child domain leaves both organizations, for example because it
   is sold, the parent removes its confirmation no later than the
   transfer (Section 5.2), and the child's new operator replaces or
   removes the claim.

5.4.  Organizations with Several Levels

   A parent domain can itself be the child domain of another
   affiliation, for example when a subsidiary's primary domain is
   affiliated with its group's primary domain.  Each affiliation is
   confirmed separately and means only what Section 8 states about that
   pair: a confirmation of the parent by its own parent says nothing
   about the parent's children.  Verifiers MAY repeat the procedure with
   the parent domain as the child domain.  A verifier that does so MUST
   NOT follow more than two levels beyond the first parent and MUST stop
   if a domain repeats.

6.  Verification

6.1.  Inputs and Outputs

   The input is a child domain in canonical form (Section 4.1).
   Verifiers that accept names containing non-ASCII characters convert
   them to A-labels as specified in [RFC5891]; the processing of [UTS46]
   with non-transitional processing is a common way of doing so.  A name
   that cannot be converted yields "permerror".








Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 14]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


   The output is a result (Section 6.3) and, when available, the parent
   domain, the channel, whether the result is authenticated
   (Section 6.5), whether the confirmation covers names below the child
   domain (Section 4.3), the parent's mode and abuse contact, and the
   time for which the result may be reused (Section 6.6).

6.2.  Procedure

   Verifiers MUST use a DNS interface that exposes the answer section of
   responses, so that CNAME [RFC1034] and DNAME [RFC6672] records in an
   answer can be detected.  In the steps below, "no record" means that
   the query returned NXDOMAIN, or NOERROR with no DAC record; this
   includes a name that exists only as an empty non-terminal.  NXDOMAIN
   and NOERROR without data are treated alike because servers differ in
   which they return.  Other DNS outcomes are handled as described in
   Section 6.4.

   1.  Query TXT at "_affiliation." followed by the child domain.

       *  No record: the result is "none".

       *  More than one DAC record, or a malformed one: "permerror".

       *  Neither a "p" nor a "t" tag: the record is only a marker, and
          the result is "none".

       *  Only one of "p" and "t", or "p" equal to the child domain:
          "permerror".

   2.  Query TXT at "_affiliation." followed by the parent domain.  Any
       "p" and "t" tags in the marker belong to the parent's own claim
       and are ignored here.  Apply the following checks in order.

       a.  The answer contains a CNAME or DNAME record: "permerror".

       b.  No record: "not-confirmed".

       c.  More than one DAC record, or a malformed one: "permerror".

       d.  A "c" value not supported by the verifier: "not-confirmed".

   3.  If the channel is "dns", query TXT at the token, followed by
       "._affiliation.", followed by the parent domain.

       *  No record: "not-confirmed".

       *  More than one DAC record, or a malformed one: "permerror".




Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 15]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


       If the channel is "https", obtain the confirmation as described
       in Section 7.

   4.  Examine the confirmation's "d" tag.

       *  Missing: "permerror".

       *  Not equal to the child domain: "not-confirmed".

       *  Equal to the child domain, but the confirmation has an "x" tag
          and the time of verification is after the end of that day:
          "not-confirmed".

       *  Otherwise: "confirmed".  If the confirmation has "s=subtree",
          the verifier reports that the result covers names below the
          child domain.

   Domain names are compared as octet strings in canonical form.  The
   procedure does not query any other names, follow references found in
   record content, or consult a list of public suffixes.

6.3.  Results

   confirmed:  The parent confirms the child.

   not-confirmed:  The child claims a parent, and the verifier did not
      obtain a confirmation.  This covers a parent that does not
      participate, an ended affiliation, a copied or fabricated claim,
      and a channel the verifier does not support.

   none:  The child makes no claim.

   temperror:  A temporary failure prevented a result; the verifier can
      retry later.

   permerror:  A record is malformed or the configuration is invalid;
      the result will not change until a record is corrected.

   Only "confirmed" establishes an affiliation.

6.4.  DNS Errors

   Any response code other than NOERROR and NXDOMAIN, a timeout, or a
   truncated response that cannot be retried over TCP yields
   "temperror".  A response that fails DNSSEC validation ("bogus"
   [RFC4035]) also yields "temperror" and MUST NOT contribute to a
   "confirmed" result.  Verifiers MUST support DNS over TCP.




Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 16]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


6.5.  Authenticated Results

   For the DNS channel, a result is authenticated if all DNS answers
   used were validated as secure [RFC4035], either by the verifier or by
   a validating resolver reached over a trusted path.

   For the HTTPS channel, the Web PKI authenticates the confirmation,
   but the decision to use that channel was taken from the marker.  The
   result is authenticated only if the claim and the marker were
   validated as secure.  Otherwise, a forged marker could select the
   HTTPS channel for a parent that does not use it (Section 11.5).

   Verifiers report whether a result is authenticated.  The abuse
   contact is taken from the marker and is authenticated only when the
   marker is.

6.6.  Caching

   A verifier MAY reuse a result for the smallest of: the TTLs [RFC2181]
   of the DNS answers used, where the TTL of a negative answer is
   determined as in [RFC2308]; the freshness lifetime of the HTTPS
   response (Section 7.2); the time remaining until a confirmation's
   expiry; and 86400 seconds.

   After "temperror", verifiers retry with increasing intervals and
   SHOULD NOT retry more often than once a minute.  Resolvers that serve
   stale data [RFC8767] can extend the time for which an ended
   affiliation still verifies.

7.  HTTPS Channel

   A parent whose marker contains "c=https" publishes confirmations as
   HTTPS resources instead of DNS records, which suits operators that
   manage such data through a web application rather than their DNS
   provider.  The claim and the marker remain DNS records; selecting
   this channel is done in the parent's DNS zone.

7.1.  Resource

   The confirmation for a token is the resource identified by:

   https://<parent domain>/.well-known/affiliation/<token>

   A confirmed token is served with status code 200 and media type
   "text/plain".  The content is one DAC confirmation record, with the
   same syntax as in the DNS, optionally followed by a single CRLF or
   LF:




Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 17]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


   Request:
     GET https://bank.example/.well-known/affiliation/
                 mfrggzdfmztwq2lknnwg23tpobyxe43u
     Accept: text/plain

   Response:
     200 OK
     Content-Type: text/plain
     Cache-Control: max-age=3600

     v=DAC1; d=embank.example

   A token that is not confirmed is answered with 404 (Not Found) or 410
   (Gone).

7.2.  Fetching

   A verifier fetching a confirmation:

   *  MUST use the "https" scheme on port 443, with the parent domain as
      the host, and MUST validate the server's certificate for that name
      [RFC9110] [RFC9525];

   *  MUST NOT follow redirects;

   *  MUST NOT connect to addresses that the IANA special-purpose
      address registries [RFC6890] mark as not globally reachable, and
      MUST connect to the address it checked;

   *  MUST NOT send cookies or other credentials; and

   *  MUST read no more than 1024 octets of content and SHOULD limit the
      request to 10 seconds.

   Responses are handled as follows:

   *  200 with media type "text/plain" (parameters are ignored) and
      content that is exactly one DAC record: the record is the
      confirmation, examined as in step 4 of Section 6.2.  A malformed
      record, other content, another media type, or more than 1024
      octets yields "permerror".

   *  404 or 410: "not-confirmed".

   *  5xx, a connection failure, a timeout, or a TLS failure:
      "temperror".





Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 18]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


   *  Any other status code, including 3xx, or a parent domain that has
      no globally reachable address: "permerror".

   The freshness lifetime of a response is zero if it carries Cache-
   Control no-store or no-cache, is otherwise taken from a max-age
   directive if present, and is otherwise 3600 seconds.

8.  Meaning of a Confirmation

   This section states what a "confirmed" result means, so that relying
   parties can use it consistently.  It does not prescribe relying-party
   policy.

8.1.  What a Confirmation States

   A "confirmed" result means that, at the time of verification, the
   parent operator had published a statement that:

   1.  the child domain is operated by the parent operator or by a party
       acting under its authority; and

   2.  reports of abuse concerning the child domain may be directed to
       the parent operator, at the contact in the marker or by its usual
       means.

   With "s=subtree", both statements also apply to every name below the
   child domain.

   The second element distinguishes an affiliation from a mere
   association: a parent that confirms a child is the expected recipient
   of complaints about it.  The statement is made by whoever controls
   the parent domain's DNS zone, or its HTTPS origin when that channel
   is used.

8.2.  What a Confirmation Does Not State

   A confirmation says nothing about the trustworthiness, identity, or
   licensing of the parent operator, or about the content served by the
   child.  A confirmation from a parent domain registered yesterday is
   as valid as one from a long-established organization.  A confirmation
   therefore conveys at most the standing that a relying party already
   attributes to the parent domain.









Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 19]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


8.3.  Considerations for Relying Parties

   *  Takedown decisions.  When a domain resembling a protected name is
      confirmed as affiliated with a parent that the relying party
      already regards as legitimate, the resemblance is unlikely to be
      an impersonation of the parent's own name, and a conflict with
      another organization's name is a dispute between organizations.
      Relying parties can direct such reports to the parent's abuse
      contact or to the dispute processes for name conflicts.  A
      confirmation alone does not justify delaying action on abuse that
      has been observed (Section 11.9).

   *  Reputation.  A relying party may extend to a confirmed child some
      or all of the reputation it attributes to the parent.  Attributing
      more standing to the child than to the parent is inconsistent with
      the meaning above, and taking abuse observed on a confirmed child
      into account when assessing the parent gives parent operators a
      reason to confirm only domains they control.

   *  Testing mode.  Confirmations from a parent in "test" mode are
      intended for reporting to the parent, not for decisions.

   *  Other results.  "none", "not-confirmed", "temperror", and
      "permerror" are not, by themselves, evidence that a domain is
      malicious.  Most domains publish no claim.  A claim that a well-
      known parent does not confirm may be an attempt to borrow its
      standing, but it may also result from misconfiguration or from
      records an attacker caused to appear in a victim's zone
      (Section 11.10).

   *  Assurance.  Relying parties taking consequential actions can
      consider whether a result was authenticated (Section 6.5) and, for
      unauthenticated results, the measures in Section 11.5.

9.  Failure Reports

   A parent can ask verifiers to report claims that name it but are not
   confirmed, for example by a domain that copied a claim or fabricated
   one.  Such reports let the parent learn about attempts to borrow its
   standing, and about confirmations that have expired or been removed
   by mistake.  Sending reports is optional for verifiers.










Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 20]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


9.1.  When Reports Are Sent

   A verifier MAY send a report when it obtained the parent's marker,
   the marker carries an "f" tag, and the result is "not-confirmed" or
   "permerror" for a reason arising after step 2 of Section 6.2, that
   is, a missing, mismatched, expired, or malformed confirmation, or a
   confirmation that could not be obtained over HTTPS.  Reports are not
   sent for "temperror" or for an unsupported channel.  Reports are
   particularly useful while a parent is in "test" mode, and verifiers
   may send them then.

   A verifier MUST NOT send a report to an "f" URI that does not meet
   the conditions in Section 4.4; in particular, reports go only to the
   parent domain or names below it, which prevents a marker from
   directing reports at third parties.  A parent that wants reports
   processed by a provider points a name below its domain, or its mail
   routing, at that provider.  Because an attacker can create any number
   of child domains, a verifier MUST send at most one message per report
   destination in 24 hours, combining reports into it, and SHOULD limit
   the number of reports per message, for example to 1000.

9.2.  Report Format

   A report is a JSON [RFC8259] array of objects, each with the
   following members:

   version:  The string "DAC1".

   observed:  The time of verification, as an RFC 3339 date-time
      [RFC3339] in UTC.

   child:  The child domain, in canonical form.

   parent:  The parent domain, in canonical form.

   result:  "not-confirmed" or "permerror".

   reason:  One of the values in the "DAC Report Reasons" registry
      (Section 13.3.3).

   reporter (optional):  A domain name identifying the organization
      sending the report.









Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 21]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


   [
     {
       "version": "DAC1",
       "observed": "2026-10-22T09:30:00Z",
       "child": "embank-login.example",
       "parent": "bank.example",
       "result": "not-confirmed",
       "reason": "different-child",
       "reporter": "filter.example"
     }
   ]

   Receivers ignore members and reason values they do not recognize.

9.3.  Delivery

   For an "https" URI, the verifier sends the report in an HTTP POST
   request with media type "application/json", subject to the same
   restrictions as in Section 7.2, except that the content may be up to
   65536 octets.  For a "mailto" URI, the verifier sends an email
   message with the subject "DAC report for" followed by the parent
   domain, whose body, or one of whose body parts, is the report with
   media type "application/json".  Verifiers SHOULD send report email
   from a domain that authenticates its mail, so that receivers can
   filter it.  Failure to deliver a report is not retried more than
   once.

9.4.  Handling Reports

   Reports are unauthenticated assertions by whoever sent them.  A
   parent treats a report as a prompt to verify the named child itself,
   not as evidence, and rate-limits what it accepts.  Reports are not a
   substitute for the monitoring described in Section 10.7.

10.  Operational Considerations

10.1.  Automation and Inventory

   Unless an expiry date is used, the records contain no dates, so
   creating an affiliation is a one-time operation with nothing to
   renew.  An organization with many domains maintains the records with
   a tool that generates tokens and writes records through its DNS
   providers' interfaces.








Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 22]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


   Affiliations go stale when domains change hands.  The most reliable
   control is to derive confirmations from the organization's registrar
   accounts or domain inventory, so that a confirmation is removed when
   its domain leaves the inventory.  Registrars and DNS providers can
   offer this as a service.

10.2.  TTLs, Signatures, and Revocation Time

   The time for an ended affiliation to stop verifying is normally
   bounded by the TTL of the confirmation (or the freshness lifetime of
   the HTTPS confirmation) and by the 86400-second limit in Section 6.6;
   serve-stale resolvers and replay can extend it (Section 6.6,
   Section 11.6).  A TTL of 3600 seconds is a reasonable default.  For
   signed zones, the validity period of signatures over confirmation
   records also bounds how long a deleted confirmation can be replayed
   (Section 11.6); parents wanting prompt revocation keep it short for
   names below "_affiliation".  The negative-caching TTL of the parent
   zone affects how soon a new confirmation becomes visible.

10.3.  Staged Deployment

   A parent can publish its marker with "m=test", create confirmations,
   and check results before changing the marker to "enforce".  Relying
   parties can likewise verify in a reporting-only mode before acting on
   results.

10.4.  Choosing and Changing Channels

   The DNS channel needs only DNS records and benefits from DNSSEC.  The
   HTTPS channel suits parents that prefer to manage confirmations in a
   web application; it requires that the parent domain itself serve
   HTTPS on port 443 without redirecting, which many organizations' apex
   domains do not do by default.  A parent that changes channel
   publishes confirmations in the new channel before changing the marker
   and removes the old ones after the marker's TTL has passed.

   Confirmations may be hosted by a DNS provider through CNAME records
   or by delegating the "_affiliation" name; the provider can then
   create and remove confirmations (Section 11.7).

10.5.  Load and Scale

   Verification costs at most three TXT queries.  Markers are shared by
   all children of a parent and are cached accordingly.  Most domains
   publish no claim, and for them the cost is one query.  A parent using
   the HTTPS channel receives a request for each verification of one of
   its children that is not answered from a cache; such parents SHOULD
   serve confirmations from infrastructure able to handle that load and



Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 23]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


   SHOULD set a Cache-Control max-age.

10.6.  DNS Configuration

   *  Parents SHOULD sign their zones with DNSSEC so that verifiers can
      obtain authenticated results.  A signed parent zone SHOULD use
      NSEC3 [RFC5155] with the parameters in [RFC9276], compact denial
      of existence [RFC9824], or minimally covering NSEC records
      [RFC4470], and SHOULD NOT use NSEC records that allow the zone to
      be walked (Section 12).

   *  Parents SHOULD restrict zone transfers to their own secondary
      servers [RFC5936] [RFC9103].

   *  Parents and children MUST NOT publish wildcard records at or below
      their "_affiliation" names, and SHOULD NOT publish wildcard CNAME
      records that cover those names.

10.7.  Monitoring

   Parent operators can check newly observed domains, for example from
   Certificate Transparency logs, for claims naming their domain.  A
   claim carrying a token the parent did not issue indicates a domain
   attempting to borrow its standing; a claim for a domain no longer in
   the parent's inventory indicates a confirmation that should already
   have been removed.

11.  Security Considerations

11.1.  Threat Model

   The attacker wants a domain it controls to verify as affiliated with
   a parent it does not control, to prevent a legitimate affiliation
   from verifying, to use an affiliation to delay action against abuse,
   or to learn a parent's affiliated domains.  The attacker can register
   domains, publish records in zones it controls, place content on web
   hosting it has access to, observe public DNS data, and in some cases
   forge or replay DNS responses on the path to a verifier.  Compromise
   of the parent's DNS zone or web origin is discussed in Section 11.8.

11.2.  Fabricated Claims

   Anyone can publish a claim naming any parent.  The claim is confirmed
   only if a record exists under the parent's "_affiliation" node, or at
   the parent's HTTPS origin when the parent has selected that channel,
   that names the claiming domain.  Absent forged responses
   (Section 11.5), only the parent operator can create such a record.




Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 24]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


11.3.  Copied Claims and Token Disclosure

   Claims and tokens are public.  An attacker that copies a legitimate
   claim to its own domain obtains a confirmation naming the legitimate
   child, and the result is "not-confirmed".  The binding between token
   and child is in the parent's confirmation, which the attacker cannot
   alter.

11.4.  Stale Confirmations

   If a parent fails to remove the confirmation of a child domain that
   has changed hands, the new registrant of the child inherits the
   affiliation.  This is the most likely failure in practice.  The
   requirement in Section 5.2, inventory-driven automation
   (Section 10.1), and monitoring (Section 10.7) address it.  Relying
   parties with access to registration data [RFC9083] [RFC9224] can
   treat a confirmation with caution when the child's registration
   changed after the confirmation was first observed.

   Likewise, if a parent domain expires and is registered by someone
   else, the new registrant can confirm arbitrary domains.  Relying
   parties that attribute standing to a parent based on its history can
   reassess a parent whose registration has changed rather than carrying
   standing across the change.

11.5.  Forged DNS Responses

   An attacker able to forge DNS responses to a verifier, for example
   through cache poisoning [RFC5452] or by hijacking routes to
   authoritative servers, can produce any result for unsigned zones.
   For the DNS channel, the forgery has to cover the parent's records;
   forging the claim alone cannot produce "confirmed".

   Forging the marker can switch the channel in either direction.  In
   particular, an attacker that can forge the marker of an unsigned
   parent zone and can place a file at a path under the parent's HTTPS
   origin, for example through shared hosting or an upload feature, can
   obtain "confirmed" for any domain.  Such a result is not
   authenticated (Section 6.5), which is why the Web PKI alone does not
   make an HTTPS result authenticated.

   Results authenticated with DNSSEC are not affected by forged
   responses.  Relying parties taking consequential actions on
   unauthenticated results can perform the queries from several network
   locations and require the answers to agree, as certification
   authorities are required to do for domain validation [CABF-BR].





Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 25]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


11.6.  Replay of Deleted Records

   An attacker on the path to a verifier can replay a confirmation that
   the parent has deleted.  For unsigned zones, any response can be
   forged anyway.  For signed zones, a replayed RRset validates until
   its signatures expire, which can be weeks.  Parents that want
   deletion to take effect promptly against such attackers use short
   signature validity periods for names below "_affiliation"
   (Section 10.2).  A confirmation with an expiry date cannot be
   replayed to effect beyond that date.

11.7.  Wildcards, Aliases, and Delegation

   If a parent zone contained a wildcard such as "*.bank.example" and no
   "_affiliation" node existed, queries for confirmation names would be
   answered from the wildcard [RFC4592].  If that wildcard were a CNAME
   into a zone operated by a third party, such as a hosting platform, a
   tenant of that platform might be able to publish a TXT record there
   confirming a domain of its choosing.  The marker prevents this: since
   its owner name exists, a wildcard above it cannot match names below
   it, and verifiers reject a marker reached through a CNAME or DNAME
   record.  An empty non-terminal at "_affiliation" also blocks the
   wildcard but yields "not-confirmed" because there is no marker.

   Confirmation names may be CNAMEs, and the "_affiliation" name may be
   delegated or carry a DNAME record.  The operator of the target or
   delegated zone can then create and remove confirmations.  Parents
   delegate in this way only to parties they would trust to publish
   confirmations on their behalf.

11.8.  Compromise of the Parent

   An attacker controlling the parent's DNS zone can confirm any domain;
   it can also redirect the parent domain itself, so DAC does not add to
   the consequences of that compromise.  An attacker controlling only
   the parent's web origin can confirm domains through the HTTPS channel
   only if the parent's marker selects that channel or the marker is
   forged (Section 11.5).  Verifiers report the channel used, and
   relying parties concerned about web compromise can give less weight
   to confirmations obtained over HTTPS.  A parent can detect
   confirmations it did not create by comparing them with its inventory,
   and recovers by deleting them.

11.9.  Use of Affiliations to Deflect Takedowns

   An attacker that controls a parent domain to which relying parties
   attribute standing, for example an expired domain re-registered for
   its history or a compromised DNS account, can confirm look-alike
   domains used for phishing.  Takedown requests may then be directed to
   an abuse contact that the attacker controls.  Relying parties should
   not let a confirmation alone delay action against abuse they have
   observed, and can reassess parents whose registration has changed
   (Section 11.4) or whose set of confirmed domains grows unexpectedly.

11.10.  Inducing Adverse Results

   An attacker able to place a TXT record at "_affiliation" under a
   victim's domain, for example through a dangling or wildcard CNAME in
   the victim's zone pointing to a platform the attacker uses, controls
   the victim's claim.  It can make the victim appear to claim a parent
   that does not confirm it, which is one reason why results other than
   "confirmed" are not by themselves evidence of malice (Section 8.3).
   It can also name a parent the attacker operates and confirm the claim
   there, making the victim appear affiliated with the attacker and
   directing abuse reports about the victim to the attacker's contact.
   Children therefore MUST NOT publish wildcards covering their
   "_affiliation" name (Section 10.6) and SHOULD make their claim name a
   CNAME only to names they or their parent operator control.

11.11.  Resource Consumption

   Verification is bounded at three TXT queries and one HTTPS request
   with its address resolution, with no recursion and no references in
   record content, avoiding the lookup-limit problems experienced with
   [RFC7208]; following several levels (Section 5.4) is bounded at nine
   queries.

   An attacker can publish claims with random tokens naming a
   participating parent, so that each verification causes a query for a
   new name at the parent's authoritative servers, or a request to its
   HTTPS origin.  Aggressive use of DNSSEC-validated caches [RFC8198]
   can absorb such queries only where denial of existence covers ranges
   of names; the configurations recommended in Section 10.6 to prevent
   zone walking, and unsigned zones, give up that protection, so parents
   using them depend on verifiers limiting their query rates.  Verifiers
   that accept names to verify from untrusted sources SHOULD rate-limit
   the queries and requests they send for each parent domain.

11.12.  Subtree Confirmations

   A subtree confirmation extends the parent's statement to names that
   the parent does not list individually.  If anyone other than the
   parent's organization can create names below the child, for example
   because the child offers subdomains to customers or has a delegation
   or wildcard record pointing to a third party, those parties' names
   would appear affiliated with the parent.  A subtree confirmation is
   also not evidence about the content of any particular name below the
   child; abuse observed there is handled as in Section 11.9.  This is
   why parents include "s=subtree" only for children whose entire
   namespace they control (Section 4.3).  A dangling record below the
   child, such as a CNAME to a deprovisioned cloud resource, has the
   same effect if taken over, so parents using subtree confirmations
   keep the child's zone free of such records.

11.13.  Failure Reports

   An attacker can publish many claims naming a parent so that verifiers
   send reports to it; the per-pair limit in Section 9.1 and parent-side
   rate limiting bound the effect.  Restricting report destinations to
   the parent's own names prevents markers from being used to direct
   reports at third parties.  A forged marker can direct reports from a
   verifier to a name chosen by the attacker only within the same limits
   as other forged responses (Section 11.5).  Reports are never evidence
   that a domain is malicious (Section 9.4).

11.14.  Expiry Dates

   Expiry dates are evaluated against the verifier's clock.  An expiry
   date limits how long a forgotten confirmation remains effective but
   does not replace removing confirmations (Section 5.2).

11.15.  Confusable Names

   An attacker can create its own parent and children with names
   resembling a legitimate organization's.  Their affiliations verify,
   but convey only the standing of the attacker's parent (Section 8.2).
   User interfaces that display affiliations SHOULD display names in a
   form that makes confusable characters evident [UTS39].

12.  Privacy Considerations

   A claim publicly discloses that the child declares an affiliation
   with the parent; this disclosure is the purpose of the claim.  The
   parent publishes no list of its domains: a query at the parent
   reveals nothing about which children exist, and confirmations can be
   located only from the corresponding claims.  A third party can still
   find affiliated domains by examining domains one by one, for example
   those listed in Certificate Transparency logs or zone files, so
   organizations should assume that every domain publishing a claim is
   discoverable.  Domains that publish no claim, such as names held
   defensively or not yet launched, are not revealed.

   This property depends on confirmation names not being enumerable.
   Zone transfers and DNSSEC denial of existence using NSEC records can
   reveal them; NSEC3 hashes of names containing random tokens of 128
   bits or more cannot feasibly be reversed.  Section 10.6 gives
   recommendations.

   Queries reveal to resolver operators, and to passive DNS collections,
   which domains a verifier is evaluating.  QNAME minimisation [RFC9156]
   reduces exposure of full names to servers higher in the DNS tree.

   Failure reports disclose to the parent which of the verifier's
   evaluations involved claims naming the parent.  Verifiers that do not
   wish to disclose this do not send reports, and the reports contain no
   information about why an evaluation took place.

   The abuse contact in the marker is published, and parents SHOULD use
   role addresses.  Publishers SHOULD NOT include personal data in
   records.

Appendix A.  Design Rationale

A.1.  Why TXT Records

   [RFC5507] discourages overloading TXT records, mainly because
   unrelated uses collide at the same owner name.  DAC uses a dedicated
   underscored name [RFC8552] and a version tag, which avoid that
   collision.  A dedicated resource record type would remove the need
   for version-tag parsing, and authoritative servers handle unknown
   types [RFC3597].  TXT was chosen because DNS provisioning interfaces
   used by domain operators support it universally, and because
   experience with the dedicated SPF type [RFC6686], which [RFC7208]
   removed, showed the deployment cost of requiring one.  The data model
   is simple enough that a dedicated type could be added later without
   changing it.

A.2.  Why Not a List of Domains

   If the parent published a list of its domains, a single query at the
   parent would disclose the organization's entire portfolio at once,
   including domains not otherwise easy to find, which is a ready-made
   target list for attackers and can reveal products before launch.
   Every new domain would require editing a shared record, and the list
   would be bounded by record size or moved to a separate document
   fetched over HTTPS, adding a dependency.  Hashing list entries does
   not help, because domain names are guessable.  Per-child
   confirmations reveal nothing at the parent and are each located only
   from a claim.

A.3.  Why Not Signatures

   A parent could publish a public key and sign each child's name, with
   the signature published by the child, so that the parent's zone would
   not change per child.  Signatures remain valid after a child changes
   hands, however, so they need expiry dates, periodic re-signing of
   every child, revocation lists for urgent cases, and procedures for
   key rotation and compromise.  DAC instead relies on the parent's
   existing control of its DNS zone or web origin, at the cost of one
   record per child at the parent.

A.4.  Why the Procedure Does Not Use the Public Suffix List

   Mechanisms that infer relationships between names depend on the
   Public Suffix List [PSL], a community-maintained list without a
   standard update model; [RFC9989] replaced its use in DMARC for this
   reason.  DAC infers nothing: each confirmation requires a record that
   only the parent's operator can publish.  A claim naming a public
   suffix as its parent is confirmed only if that suffix's operator



Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 39]

Internet-Draft       Domain Affiliation Confirmation        October 2026


   confirms it.  Relying parties may still use boundary information to
   decide which names to evaluate and how to attribute standing.

A.5.  Why Affiliation Is Not Transitive Across Organizations

   A mechanism in which organizations vouch for one another, with
   vouching that propagates, lets an attacker gain standing by
   compromising or paying any participant, dilutes the meaning of each
   step, and makes revocation depend on parties who may not know about
   the chain.  DAC expresses only that one operator operates or
   authorizes a domain.  The protocol cannot tell whether two levels
   belong to the same organization, which is why a confirmation says
   nothing about the confirmed domain's own children (Section 5.4).

A.6.  Why One Parent per Child

   In designs where every member of a group lists, and must be confirmed
   by, every other member, each new domain requires changes at every
   member, and a verifier must compute the group as the closure of
   pairwise links, at a cost that grows with the size of the group and
   has to be capped by arbitrary limits.  DAC relates each child to
   exactly one parent, so adding a domain changes two records and
   verification has a fixed cost.  Organizations with several levels are
   handled one affiliation at a time (Section 5.4).

A.7.  Why Authentication Is Reported Rather Than Required

   Requiring DNSSEC or an HTTPS origin on both sides of every
   relationship would exclude most domains, including names held
   defensively that serve no content, and would make the mechanism
   unusable for most of the organizations it is meant to help.  DAC
   instead reports whether each result is authenticated (Section 6.5),
   so that relying parties can require authentication where their
   decisions warrant it, and states precisely when an HTTPS confirmation
   counts as authenticated.

A.8.  Why No Aggregators

   DAC needs no index, registry of relationships, or submission service.
   Every verifier obtains results directly from the domains concerned,
   so there is no additional party to operate, trust, or keep current,
   and no endpoint that can be used to make a service send traffic to
   arbitrary domains.  Aggregators can be built on DAC, but nothing
   depends on them.

A.9.  Why the Meaning of a Confirmation Is Defined

   A mechanism that declares a relationship between domains, but whose
   specification forbids relying parties to draw conclusions from it,
   gives those parties nothing to act on, and the problems in
   Section 1.1 remain.  DAC addresses a specific operational problem
   with intended consumers, takedown services and reputation systems,
   and states what a confirmation means (Section 8), including where
   reports of abuse are to be sent.  It also bounds that meaning: a
   confirmation conveys no more standing than the parent already has
   (Section 8.2).  The author invites operators of such services to
   comment on whether the stated meaning meets their needs.

Appendix B.  Complete Example

   The operator of bank.example launches embank.example and uses the DNS
   channel.

   ; In the bank.example zone (once):
   _affiliation.bank.example.   3600 IN TXT
       "v=DAC1; r=mailto:abuse@bank.example"

   ; In the bank.example zone (per child):
   mfrggzdfmztwq2lknnwg23tpobyxe43u._affiliation.bank.example.
                                3600 IN TXT "v=DAC1; d=embank.example"

   ; In the embank.example zone:
   _affiliation.embank.example. 3600 IN TXT
       "v=DAC1; p=bank.example; t=mfrggzdfmztwq2lknnwg23tpobyxe43u"

   A verifier evaluating embank.example:

   1.  queries TXT at _affiliation.embank.example and obtains parent
       "bank.example" and the token;

   2.  queries TXT at _affiliation.bank.example and obtains the marker:
       channel "dns", mode "enforce", contact
       "mailto:abuse@bank.example";

   3.  queries TXT at
       mfrggzdfmztwq2lknnwg23tpobyxe43u._affiliation.bank.example and
       obtains "d=embank.example", which equals the child domain.

   The result is "confirmed", reusable for 3600 seconds, and
   authenticated if all three answers validate as secure.






Manelidis                 Expires 25 April 2027                [Page 41]

   A mail filter assessing "statements.embank.example" verifies
   "embank.example", the domain it already treats as responsible for
   that name.  Because the confirmation above has no "s" tag, the result
   states nothing about "statements.embank.example" itself.  If
   bank.example operates every name under embank.example, it publishes
   the confirmation as "v=DAC1; d=embank.example; s=subtree", and the
   result covers "statements.embank.example" too.

   An attacker registers embank-login.example and publishes a copy of
   the claim.  Step 3 returns "d=embank.example", which differs from
   "embank-login.example", so the result is "not-confirmed".  When the
   operator of bank.example sells embank.example, it deletes the
   confirmation; from then on, embank.example yields "not-confirmed"
   whatever its new operator publishes.
